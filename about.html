Why this exists

Most security training happens in perfect scenarios: one vulnerable machine, one intended path and a hint when you get stuck. Then the first real incident arrives and none of that applies.

Real work is messy. Logs are incomplete, alerts lie, documentation is outdated and nobody tells you where to look. The people who do well are the ones who learned to work in that mess.

Blindma1den Hack&Code trains exactly that. It’s not comfortable, and it’s not supposed to be. This field rewards people who really want to be part of it, and we want to help them get there.

How to contribute

You don’t need to be a senior to help. You need to be willing to do the work.

  • Design a lab

    Turn an incident you lived (anonymized) into a scenario others can train on.

  • Write code

    The platform is open source. Issues labeled for first-time contributors are a good place to start.

  • Translate

    The site is in English and Spanish. Help us reach more people in their own language.

  • Publish write-ups

    Explain how you solved a lab or a CTF challenge. The best ones get published in Briefing.

  • Report bugs

    Platform bugs go to GitHub. Security vulnerabilities go through the disclosure policy.

  • Spread the word

    Tell the people who are bored of clean labs. They’ll thank you later. Probably.
